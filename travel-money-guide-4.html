Travel Guide

Travel Money
Guide.

Plan a realistic budget, handle currency and payments smartly, and avoid the money mistakes that catch travellers out.

Plan ahead

Budget Planning

A realistic budget is the foundation of a stress-free trip. Estimate honestly, leave a buffer, and track as you go.

Estimate expenses
  • Break costs into transport, stay, food, and activities
  • Research typical prices for your destination
  • Add visas, insurance, and local transport
Build a realistic budget
  • Set a daily spending figure and a total cap
  • Separate fixed costs from flexible ones
  • Always keep a buffer for the unexpected
Avoid overspending
  • Track spending daily with a notes app
  • Set aside money for big-ticket items first
  • Adjust as you go, not at the end
Get fair value

Currency Exchange

Exchange rates and fees quietly add up. A little awareness keeps more of your money in your pocket.

Understand rates
  • Check the real mid-market rate before exchanging
  • The advertised rate often hides a margin
  • Compare a few options rather than the first one
Where to exchange
  • Airport counters usually offer the worst rates
  • Banks and reputable exchanges tend to be fairer
  • ATMs can be convenient but check the fees
Get better value
  • Exchange a little before you go, the rest there
  • Avoid exchanging large sums in one risky spot
  • Always pay in the local currency, not your own
How you pay

Payment Methods

Each method has its place. Carrying a mix means you're covered wherever you are and whatever happens.

Cash
Essential for small vendors, markets, tips, and places that don't take cards. Carry a modest amount, never your whole budget.
Debit cards
Handy for ATM withdrawals and everyday spending. Check foreign fees and daily limits before you travel.
Credit cards
Useful for larger bookings and often include protections. Keep one as a backup, stored separately from your main card.
Digital wallets
Increasingly accepted and convenient. Make sure your phone is set up and that the wallet works at your destination.
International options
Acceptance varies widely by country. Research what's common at your destination so you're never caught without a working option.
Carry a mix
The safest approach is never relying on one method. A blend of cash and cards keeps you covered if one fails or is lost.
Spend less, do more

Saving Money While Traveling

Cutting costs doesn't mean cutting the experience. Small choices across the trip add up to real savings.

Accommodation
  • Travel in shoulder season for lower prices
  • Compare stay types, not just hotels
  • Look slightly outside the busiest areas
Transportation
  • Use public transport over taxis where you can
  • Book longer journeys ahead for better fares
  • Walk short distances and see more on the way
Food
  • Eat where locals eat for value and flavour
  • Markets and street food beat tourist spots
  • Self-cater some meals if you have a kitchen
Activities
  • Mix paid attractions with free experiences
  • Look for city passes if you'll do a lot
  • Book popular activities ahead for better prices
Learn from others

Common Money Mistakes

Most travel-money problems are avoidable. Watch out for these common slip-ups.

Carrying too much cash
Large amounts of cash are a target and a single point of failure. Carry what you need and secure the rest.
Ignoring exchange fees
Poor rates and hidden fees eat into your budget. Always check what you're really paying before exchanging.
No emergency fund
Unexpected costs happen. Without a reserve, a small problem can derail a whole trip.
Relying on one card
If your only card is lost or blocked, you're stuck. Always carry a backup, stored separately.
Forgetting to notify your bank
Foreign transactions can trigger a freeze. Tell your bank your travel dates before you go.
Paying in your home currency
When asked, choosing your home currency abroad usually means a worse rate. Pay in the local currency instead.
If things go wrong

Emergency Money Tips

Knowing the steps in advance turns a money emergency into a manageable problem. Stay calm and act quickly.

01
Lost or stolen card
Freeze or cancel it immediately through your banking app or the card-blocking number you saved. Then switch to your backup card.
02
Cash stolen
Report it to local police for any insurance claim, and use your separately stored emergency cash and backup card to keep going.
03
Unexpected expenses
Lean on your buffer fund first. If needed, a credit card can bridge a gap, but keep track so it doesn't snowball.
04
Need funds fast
Money-transfer services let trusted contacts send funds quickly. Know how they work before you travel, just in case.

Keep a money emergency note. Save your card-blocking numbers, bank contacts, and a trusted contact back home — stored separately from your phone and wallet.

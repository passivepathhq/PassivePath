Travel Guide

Safe Travel
Tips.

Practical, calm advice for staying safe on the road — your body, your belongings, your data, and what to do if something goes wrong.

Stay aware

Personal Safety

Most trips are safe. A few sensible habits keep small risks from becoming real problems — and let you travel with confidence.

Stay aware
  • Keep your head up and phone away in busy areas
  • Know the route before you set off
  • Trust your instincts — leave anything that feels off
Protect valuables
  • Split cash and cards across bag, wallet, and room
  • Use a hidden pouch or money belt in crowds
  • Keep bags zipped and in front of you
Avoid unsafe spots
  • Research areas to avoid before you arrive
  • Stick to well-lit, populated streets at night
  • Don't broadcast expensive items or plans
Travel confidently
  • Walk like you know where you're going
  • Share your itinerary with someone you trust
  • Keep your phone charged and reachable
Secure your room
  • Use the door lock and safe where available
  • Keep valuables out of sight
  • Note the nearest exits when you check in
Blend in
  • Dress in line with local norms
  • Be discreet with maps and cameras
  • Learn a few words of the local language
Feel your best

Health & Wellness

Staying well on the road is mostly preparation. Sort the basics before you go and look after yourself once you're there.

Vaccinations
  • Check recommended vaccines well ahead of travel
  • Carry a record of what you've had
  • Ask a travel clinic about your destination
Food & water
  • Drink safe or bottled water where advised
  • Eat freshly cooked, hot food when unsure
  • Be cautious with ice and raw items
Medications
  • Bring enough for the whole trip, plus a little extra
  • Keep a copy of your prescription
  • Carry medicines in your hand luggage
Hydration
  • Carry a reusable bottle and refill often
  • Drink more in heat and at altitude
  • Watch caffeine and alcohol in the sun
Emergency prep
  • Know where the nearest clinic or hospital is
  • Save local emergency numbers
  • Make sure your insurance covers your activities
Rest & pace
  • Adjust gradually to new time zones
  • Don't over-schedule the first day
  • Sleep and sun protection matter more than you think
Protect your data

Digital Security

Your phone holds your money, bookings, and identity. A few habits keep it — and your data — safe abroad.

Public Wi-Fi
  • Avoid banking or sensitive logins on open networks
  • Use a VPN for an extra layer of protection
  • Prefer your own mobile data for anything important
Device security
  • Lock devices with a strong PIN or biometrics
  • Turn on remote-find and remote-wipe
  • Keep software updated before you leave
Online payments
  • Use trusted apps and secure connections
  • Enable transaction alerts on your cards
  • Watch for skimmers at ATMs and terminals
Backup documents
  • Store digital copies of passport and bookings
  • Keep a printed set separate from the originals
  • Email yourself the essentials as a backup
Stay alert online
  • Be wary of unexpected links and messages
  • Don't share trip details publicly in real time
  • Log out of shared or hotel computers
Two-factor
  • Turn on two-factor for key accounts
  • Keep backup codes somewhere safe
  • Note that texts may not arrive abroad
Getting around

Transportation Safety

However you move around a new place, a little care keeps the journey smooth and safe.

Public transport
  • Keep bags close on buses and trains
  • Know your stop and route in advance
  • Be extra alert in crowded carriages
Taxis
  • Use official, registered taxis
  • Agree the fare or meter before you set off
  • Sit where you feel comfortable
Ride-sharing
  • Check the car, plate, and driver match the app
  • Share your trip with a friend
  • Ride in the back when travelling alone
Rental vehicles
  • Know local driving rules and road signs
  • Inspect and photograph the vehicle first
  • Keep documents and insurance handy
Walking
  • Plan your route and stay on busy streets
  • Cross where locals do and watch traffic flow
  • Avoid distractions like headphones at night
Know your spot
  • Drop a pin on your accommodation
  • Download offline maps before you explore
  • Carry the address written in the local language
Respect the place

Local Laws & Culture

A little respect goes a long way. Understanding local norms keeps you safe, welcome, and out of trouble.

Customs & etiquette
  • Learn common greetings and gestures
  • Watch how locals behave and follow their lead
  • Ask before photographing people
Dress codes
  • Cover up at religious and sacred sites
  • Pack something modest just in case
  • Respect local standards over personal style
Important laws
  • Research laws that differ sharply from home
  • Know the rules on photography and drones
  • Be aware of restrictions on certain items
Before you visit
  • Read your government's travel advice
  • Note local tipping and payment norms
  • Learn a few polite phrases in the language
If things go wrong

Emergency Preparedness

Knowing the steps ahead of time turns a stressful moment into a manageable one. Stay calm and work through it.

01
Lost passport
Report it to local police and contact your embassy or consulate right away. Your backup copies make replacement far faster.
02
Theft
Get a police report for insurance, cancel affected cards immediately, and use your spare cash and backup card.
03
Medical emergency
Call local emergency services, head to the nearest hospital, and notify your travel insurer as soon as you can.
04
Need assistance
Your embassy can help with serious issues abroad. Save its contact details and location before you travel.

Keep an emergency card. Write down local emergency numbers, your embassy, your insurer's hotline, and a trusted contact back home — and keep it separate from your phone.
